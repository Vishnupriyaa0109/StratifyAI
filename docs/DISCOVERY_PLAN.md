# Product discovery plan

**Important:** This is a proposed research plan. No customer interviews or user validation are claimed in this repository.

## Research objective
Test whether product managers and adjacent customer-facing roles struggle to convert fragmented customer feedback into prioritised, measurable product decisions — and learn which part of that workflow is most valuable.

## Research questions
1. How is feedback collected today, and where does it live?
2. How are recurring themes identified and verified?
3. How do teams decide which problems enter the roadmap?
4. What evidence is needed to trust a prioritisation decision?
5. How are experiments and post-launch outcomes recorded?
6. What integrations or data controls are required before using a new tool?

## Participant hypotheses
Recruit 8–12 participants across:
- Product managers / product owners in B2B SaaS.
- Customer success or support leaders.
- Founders or product leads at small software teams.

Aim for a mix of company sizes and maturity. This is a proposed sample, not completed recruitment.

## Interview guide (30–40 minutes)
1. Tell me about the last time customer feedback changed a product decision.
2. Walk me through how feedback arrived and how you consolidated it.
3. What part took the most time or caused disagreement?
4. How do you distinguish a one-off request from a recurring problem?
5. How do you estimate reach, impact, confidence, and effort?
6. How do you explain why one problem was prioritised over another?
7. What happens after a feature is shipped? Which metric is reviewed?
8. What tools are involved, and what information cannot leave those systems?
9. May I see an anonymised example of the current workflow?
10. What would make a new workflow clearly better or not worth adopting?

Avoid pitching StratifyAI until the participant has described their existing behaviour. Ask for examples and recent incidents, not hypothetical enthusiasm.

## Prototype usability tasks
Ask participants to:
- Find the most concerning feedback theme.
- Inspect the original records behind the theme.
- Create an opportunity from a feedback signal.
- Explain a RICE score and change one assumption.
- Create an experiment with a clear success metric.

Observe task completion, hesitation, errors, workarounds, and trust concerns. Do not lead participants to the intended answer.

## Evidence capture template
| Field | Record |
|---|---|
| Participant code | Anonymous identifier |
| Role / company stage | Only non-identifying context |
| Recent example | What happened, when, and why it mattered |
| Current workflow | Tools, handoffs, and workarounds |
| Pain intensity | Participant-described frequency and consequence |
| Existing alternatives | What already works |
| Quote / evidence | Anonymised and permissioned |
| Product implication | Opportunity or question, not a conclusion |
| Confidence | Low / medium / high, with rationale |

## Synthesis approach
1. Code observed behaviours and pains, not feature requests alone.
2. Cluster similar evidence into problem themes.
3. Keep contradictory evidence visible.
4. Rank opportunities by frequency, severity, and strategic fit; do not rely on interview counts alone.
5. Update product assumptions and document decisions.
6. Re-test the riskiest assumption with a prototype or concierge workflow.

## Research ethics and privacy
Obtain consent, minimise collected personal data, avoid recording confidential customer details, anonymise notes, and use approved storage. Never place identifiable interview data in this public demo repository.
