# Opportunity prioritisation framework

## Why RICE
The MVP uses RICE to make prioritisation assumptions explicit and comparable. It is a conversation aid, not an automatic decision-maker.

`RICE = Reach × Impact × Confidence ÷ Effort`

## Scoring guidance for a team calibration session

| Dimension | Working scale | Guidance |
|---|---|---|
| Reach | 1–100 users per agreed period | Define the time window and affected population consistently |
| Impact | 0.25, 0.5, 1, 2, 3 | Relative per-user impact; calibrate what each point means |
| Confidence | 0.1–1.0 | Confidence in reach and impact evidence, not confidence in delivery |
| Effort | 0.5+ person-weeks | Include design, engineering, QA, rollout, and maintenance where relevant |

The numeric ranges are implementation defaults for the demo, not universal industry standards.

## Example calculation
Illustrative opportunity:
- Reach = 80 users / month
- Impact = 2.5
- Confidence = 0.8
- Effort = 3 person-weeks

RICE = (80 × 2.5 × 0.8) ÷ 3 = **53.33**

The score only makes sense alongside the assumptions and evidence used to estimate each input.

## Decision review
Before promoting an opportunity:
- Can we link it to real source feedback?
- Which customer segments are affected?
- Is the problem recurring, severe, or strategically important?
- What evidence supports the reach estimate?
- What alternatives or non-product interventions exist?
- What is the cost of delaying the work?
- What would change our confidence?

## Guardrails
- Do not compare scores created with different reach windows or scales.
- Do not confuse reach with revenue impact.
- Do not inflate confidence to push a preferred feature.
- Do not double-count overlapping opportunities.
- Preserve qualitative evidence and dissent alongside the score.
- Re-score as assumptions change; keep a decision history in a future version.
